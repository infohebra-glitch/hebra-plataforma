For years, a green label and the word "conscious" were enough to make a collection look better than the rest. That's over. The European Union has decided that, when it comes to environmental claims, **an adjective is worth nothing without proof behind it**.

## The rule

It is **Directive (EU) 2024/825**, known as the directive on "empowering consumers for the green transition". It amends the EU rules on unfair commercial practices and consumer rights. Member States had to write it into national law by **27 March 2026**, and the new rules must apply from **27 September 2026**.

Several countries, including Spain and France, were behind on transposition during 2026. But the direction is the same across Europe, and the risk of misleading advertising over environmental claims already existed before the directive.

## What is now banned

The directive adds to the "blacklist" of practices that are considered misleading in all circumstances:

- **Generic claims** such as "eco-friendly", "green", "environmentally friendly" or "sustainable", if the brand cannot demonstrate recognised excellent environmental performance (for example, with the EU Ecolabel).
- **In-house sustainability labels** that aren't based on a third-party certification scheme or set up by a public authority. A seal invented by the brand is no longer valid.
- **Claims about the whole product** when the improvement only concerns part of it. If only the lining is recycled, the garment can't be presented as recycled.
- **Calling a product "carbon neutral"** on the basis of offsetting emissions elsewhere.
- **Presenting a legal requirement as a distinctive feature.** Complying with the law is not a selling point.

## What you can still say

The directive doesn't ban talking about sustainability. It bans doing so without proof. What remains allowed is the **specific and verifiable**:

- "Cotton grown in Turkey, woven and dyed in Portugal, sewn in Barcelona", with the source of each fact.
- "Composition: 100% linen", which labelling law already requires.
- "We cut unsold garments by 18% compared with last season", if you can show how it was measured.

In other words: **the adjective is replaced by data**. And data can be checked.

## What it means for you

**If you buy:** be wary of standalone words and look for what can be checked. Does it say where each stage happened? Does it say who certifies it? Can you reach that information from the garment itself? If not, "sustainable" is just a word.

**If you sell:** review your labels, website and social media before someone else does. Drop in-house seals, swap adjectives for facts and keep the evidence for every claim. Small brands have an edge here: they often know the workshop and the fabric supplier personally. That closeness, told with data, is worth more than any seal.
