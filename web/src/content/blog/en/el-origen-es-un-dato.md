"Made with love in our workshop." "Carefully selected materials." "Responsible production." Every brand tells an origin story. Very few can prove it, and almost none know whether telling it does them any good.

At Hebra we think origin needs to stop being a story and become **data**: something specific, with a source, that can be measured.

## The industry knows little about itself

Fashion Revolution's *Fashion Transparency Index* reviews each year how much information the largest fashion brands disclose. In its 2023 edition, covering 250 brands, the average score was **26%**. Only **129 of those 250 brands (52%)** published a list of their direct suppliers, usually the factories that sew the garments. Saying who spins, weaves, dyes or grows the fibre was far less common.

In other words, even the world's biggest brands usually tell, at most, the last link in the chain. It isn't bad faith. A garment's supply chain is long, and each stage is in the hands of a different company, often in a different country.

## Five stages, five questions

Turning origin into data starts with breaking it down. For each garment:

1. **Fibre:** where was it grown or made?
2. **Spinning:** where was the yarn made?
3. **Fabric:** where was the cloth made?
4. **Dyeing and finishing:** where was it dyed?
5. **Making:** where was it cut and sewn?

And for each answer, a second question almost nobody asks: **who says so?**

## Three levels of certainty

Not all data is equal, and it's fine to say so. We use three levels:

- **Declared:** the brand states it based on what it knows (for example, the supplier told it where the fabric comes from).
- **Documented:** there is paperwork behind it: an invoice, a delivery note, a supplier's certificate of origin.
- **Certified:** an independent third party has verified it against a recognised standard.

A page that says "Fabric: Portugal · documented" and "Fibre: not known yet" is far more credible than one that says "responsible origin". Being honest about what you don't know is information in itself.

## Why now

Three changes are pushing in the same direction:

- The EU **digital product passport** will require structured information about each garment, reachable from the product itself.
- **Directive 2024/825** bans generic environmental claims without proof: out goes the adjective, in comes the data.
- In France, the 2026 law requires the **place of manufacture to be shown next to the price** for online sales.

Brands that already have their origin organised by stage and by level of certainty will meet all of this with the work done.

## The question nobody measures: do your customers care?

This is what interests us most. If origin is data, you can also measure **its effect**:

- How many people open the origin section when they tap the garment?
- When: in the fitting room, while hesitating, or later at home?
- Do garments that show their origin sell better than similar ones that don't?

With that, the brand stops arguing in the abstract about whether "customers care about sustainability". It knows, with its own customers and its own garments.
