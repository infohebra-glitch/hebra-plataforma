How much clothing do you buy in a year? Most people have no idea, and that's normal: we buy garments, not kilos. But the European Environment Agency (EEA) does the maths, and the numbers help explain why Europe is changing the rules of fashion.

## What we buy

In **2022**, each person in the European Union consumed on average **19 kilos of textile products**, up from 17 kilos in 2019. That breaks down as:

- **8 kg of clothing.**
- **7 kg of household textiles** (sheets, towels, curtains…).
- **4 kg of footwear.**

It is the highest figure the agency has recorded.

## What we throw away

That same year, the EU generated **6.94 million tonnes of textile waste**: about **16 kilos per person**. And the share collected separately, for reuse or recycling, was only around **15%**. The rest, some 85 of every 100 kilos of household textile waste, ended up mixed in with general rubbish.

## What it costs

Textiles are among the consumption categories with the highest environmental impact in Europe: fifth out of twelve for raw material use, emissions, water and land, behind food, housing and transport. Per person per year, the EEA estimates around **523 kg of raw materials** and **355 kg of CO₂ equivalent** linked to the textiles we buy.

## Why the rules are changing

These figures explain much of the regulation now arriving:

- The **ban on destroying unsold clothing** (since July 2026 for large companies).
- **Extended producer responsibility** for textiles: brands will have to fund the collection and treatment of used clothing across the EU.
- The **digital product passport**, so each garment says what it is and how to repair or recycle it.
- Laws such as France's against **ultra-fast fashion**.

## What you can do with these numbers

**If you buy:** it isn't about never buying clothes again, but about buying what you'll actually wear. Knowing what a garment is made of and where helps you choose pieces that last, and look after them so they last longer.

**If you sell:** the part of the problem in your hands starts before the sale. Every garment produced and not sold is a kilo that didn't need to exist. Knowing what gets tried on and doesn't sell, and why, is the most direct way to produce better.
