The European Union has decided that products must be able to tell their story. The Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, in force since July 2024, provides for a **digital product passport**: a record reachable from the product itself with its composition, its origin and how to repair or recycle it. Textiles are among the first sectors it will apply to.

## What it is, in practice

A digital passport is structured, verifiable information linked to each product and reached from a carrier on the garment itself. Exactly what will be required will be set by the textile-specific rules, but it points to data such as:

- Material composition and the presence of substances.
- Where each stage happened: fibre, spinning and knitting, dyeing and making.
- Care, repair and end-of-life instructions.
- Certificates and environmental declarations.

## Why not wait

For a small brand, the temptation is to wait until it’s mandatory. But gathering supply-chain information takes time: you have to ask suppliers for data, organise certificates and decide what to tell and how. Brands that start early will arrive with the work done and, above all, will have learned something the rules won’t tell them: **whether telling the origin helps them sell**.

## From obligation to advantage

This is the difference between complying and benefiting. If the garment page already exists, you can measure:

- How many people open the origin chain when they tap the garment.
- In which language and at what moment: in the fitting room or at home.
- Whether garments with visible origin sell more, compared with a control group.
