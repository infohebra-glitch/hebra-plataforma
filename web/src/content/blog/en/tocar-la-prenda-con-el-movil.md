You pay with your phone at the supermarket, tap your card on the metro and probably never think about it: that's **NFC**. The same technology, built into a clothing tag, lets a garment tell you what it is and where it comes from just by bringing your phone close.

## NFC in one sentence

NFC stands for *Near Field Communication*. It lets two objects exchange a little information when they are **a few centimetres apart**. One of them (your phone) generates a small field, and the other (the tag) responds.

The tag **has no battery**. It is a tiny chip and a printed antenna, thinner than paper, that only "wakes up" when a phone comes near. That's why it can be sewn into a garment and go through the wash with it.

## How to use it

1. Hold the top of your phone (on an iPhone) or the back (on most Android phones) to the tag.
2. A notification appears on the screen.
3. Tap it and a web page opens with the garment's details.

**No app to download**, no camera, nothing to aim at. It works on iPhone XS and later without opening any app, and on the vast majority of Android phones with NFC switched on (it usually is, since it's the same feature used for payments).

## Why NFC and not a QR code

A QR code also opens a page, but you have to open the camera, focus and have decent light. In a fitting room, wearing the garment with your phone in hand, that's one step too many. NFC is **a single tap**. And a printed QR code can be copied or covered with a sticker; a tag sewn into the garment is much harder to fake.

## What NFC can't do

It's the most common question, and the answer is reassuring:

- **It can't track you.** It only works within a few centimetres, and only when you bring your phone to it. Nobody can read the tag from across the shop, let alone in the street.
- **It doesn't know who you are.** The tag only holds a garment identifier and a web address. It reads nothing from your phone.
- **It doesn't switch on by itself.** If you don't bring your phone close, nothing happens.

What happens next depends on the page that opens, as with any website. That's why it matters who is behind the page and what they do with the data.
