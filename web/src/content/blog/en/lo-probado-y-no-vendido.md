Every fashion store knows to the cent what it sells. The till records everything: which garment, which size, what time and with what discount. But the till only sees the end of the story. It doesn’t see the times a garment went into the fitting room and came back to the rail.

## The invisible half

Think of two T-shirts that sold the same last week: seven units each. In the sales report they are twins. But one was tried on eight times and the other forty-two. The first convinces almost everyone who tries it. The second attracts lots of people and loses them inside the fitting room.

These are two completely different problems, and the till data can’t tell them apart.

## What a much-tried, little-sold garment tells you

A garment with lots of try-ons and few sales has something that works: the design, the window or the price on the tag draws people in. What fails happens inside the fitting room. It is almost always one of these:

- **Size.** The pattern runs small, or the most requested size is missing.
- **Fit.** It convinces on the hanger, not on the body.
- **Price.** Once it’s on, the customer doesn’t think it’s worth the price.
- **Colour or quality.** The fabric doesn’t live up to the photo.

Each reason calls for a different decision: changing the size curve on the next order, adjusting the pattern, moving the price or not repeating the style.

## Why it matters before the sales

A much-tried, little-sold garment is a markdown waiting to happen. If you know in week three of the season, you can still act: order the missing size, change how it’s displayed or adjust the price in time. If you find out in January, all that’s left is to discount it.
