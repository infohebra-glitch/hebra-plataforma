In two years, European fashion has gone from having few environmental rules to a crowded calendar. For a brand with a handful of shops, the challenge isn't only complying: it's knowing what applies and when. Here it is, in order.

## Already in force (2026)

**19 July 2026 · No destroying unsold clothing.** The EU Ecodesign Regulation (2024/1781) bans large companies from destroying unsold clothing, accessories and footwear. Medium-sized companies follow in 2030; micro and small companies are exempt for now.

**1 September 2026 · Penalty on ultra-fast fashion in France.** The French law of 8 July 2026 applies a per-garment *malus* to ultra-fast fashion, from €0.25 to €12 in 2026.

**27 September 2026 · No more "eco" without proof.** Application date of Directive 2024/825: generic environmental claims and uncertified in-house sustainability labels are banned. Some countries, including Spain and France, were behind on transposition.

**France, in force · Product information.** France's AGEC law (Article 13) already requires brands above €10m turnover and 10,000 units a year to publish, for each model, a digital product sheet showing the country of the main stages (weaving, dyeing, making), among other data.

## 2027

**1 January 2027 · No advertising for ultra-fast fashion in France.** Advertising and influencer promotion are banned.

**February 2027 · Annual report on unsold goods.** The common EU format applies for covered companies to report how much unsold clothing they discard and why.

**17 June 2027 · Producer responsibility into national law.** Deadline for Member States to transpose Directive (EU) 2025/1892, which creates extended producer responsibility (EPR) for textiles across the EU.

**Late 2027 (planned) · Digital passport rules for textiles.** The Commission plans to adopt the act setting out what information a garment's digital passport must carry.

## 2028 – 2030

**17 April 2028 · Textile EPR up and running.** Producer responsibility schemes must be operating: brands fund the collection, sorting, reuse and recycling of the clothing they place on the market. Micro-enterprises get one extra year (April 2029).

**2029 (estimate) · First mandatory digital passport.** As a general rule, the Ecodesign Regulation sets a minimum of 18 months between adopting the rules and applying them. If they are adopted at the end of 2027, the obligation would not arrive before mid-2029. That's our calculation, not an official date.

**19 July 2030 · Medium-sized companies can't destroy either.** The ban on destroying unsold clothing extends to medium-sized companies.

**2030 · Maximum malus in France.** The ultra-fast fashion penalty rises to €2.20 – €20 per garment.

## In Spain

Spain already requires municipalities to collect textiles separately (Waste Law 7/2022). The **royal decree on textile and footwear products**, which will set out producer responsibility (producer register, joining a collective scheme, and collection and reuse targets), was notified to the European Commission in June 2026 and **had not yet been approved** at the time of writing. According to the trade press, its entry into force has been scheduled for **1 January 2029**.

## What to do now, if you're a small brand

1. **Organise your origin by garment and stage.** It's the basis for the digital passport, the French product sheet and any claim you want to make.
2. **Check your wording.** Drop "eco", "sustainable" and in-house seals if you can't back them up.
3. **Measure your surplus.** Even if the destruction ban doesn't apply to you, every leftover garment is margin and, in future, an EPR fee.
4. **Start small.** A few styles with a complete garment page teach you more than a perfect plan for the whole catalogue.
