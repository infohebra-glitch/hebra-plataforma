France is the first European country to pass a law aimed squarely at ultra-fast fashion. It is **Law no. 2026-602 of 8 July 2026**, "to reduce the environmental impact of the textile industry". It does not target fashion in general, but one specific model: thousands of new styles every week, rock-bottom prices and garments designed not to last.

Although it was passed in France, it applies to anyone selling clothes to French customers, wherever they are based. And it sets the direction other countries are likely to follow.

## What the law calls "ultra-fast fashion"

The law creates a new category in the French Environmental Code: *mode ultra-express*. It is defined by two criteria that must both be met:

- A **very high number of new styles** put on sale.
- A **weak incentive to repair** the garments.

The exact thresholds (how many styles, over what period) are to be set by a decree that, at the end of September 2026, had not yet been published. In other words, the law exists, but the precise line between who is in and who is out is still being drawn.

## Already in force: the "malus"

Since **1 September 2026**, ultra-fast fashion garments carry a penalty within the fee brands pay to fund clothing collection and recycling. According to the French government's official summary, it ranges from **€0.25 to €12 per garment in 2026**, rising to **€2.20 – €20 in 2030**, capped at 50% of the pre-tax selling price.

To put that in perspective: a €5 T-shirt can carry a penalty of a few euros. The message is clear: rock-bottom prices are no longer free.

## Coming in 2027: no more advertising

From **1 January 2027**:

- **Advertising** of ultra-fast fashion products is banned.
- So is **promotion by influencers**, paid or unpaid, with fines of up to €100,000.
- The word **"free"** can no longer be used in their campaigns.

Platforms will also have to display messages about responsible consumption, repair and recycling.

## What matters to every brand: origin next to the price

One measure goes beyond ultra-fast fashion, and we think it is the most interesting one. The law requires anyone selling clothes online to **show where the product was made, close to the price** and just as legibly.

Until now, the country of manufacture was a detail hidden on the label, or simply missing. Under this law, in France, it sits right next to the first thing a shopper looks at. That changes the conversation: once origin is visible, origin gets compared.

## And donations

The law also scraps the tax deduction these companies received for donating unsold clothing. The aim is to break a cycle in which overproducing cost almost nothing, because the surplus was donated and written off.

## What it means for you

**If you buy clothes:** you will see more information about where your clothes are made, especially from online shops selling into France. Use it. A garment that tells you its origin is standing behind something; one that doesn't is telling you something too.

**If you run a brand or a shop:** you are almost certainly not "ultra-fast". But the direction is unmistakable: Europe is moving from rewarding volume to asking for proof. Knowing where each garment comes from, how often it is tried on and how often it sells is no longer just good management; it is starting to set you apart from those who can't say.
