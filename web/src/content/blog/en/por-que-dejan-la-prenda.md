When a customer leaves the fitting room without the garment, they take the store’s most valuable information with them: the reason. Nobody writes it down. At best, a sales assistant senses it and mentions it at the end of the day.

## Asking at the right moment

Email surveys arrive late and few people answer them. Inside the fitting room, with the garment still in hand, the reason is fresh. That’s why at Hebra the question lives on the garment itself: the customer taps the tag with their phone, the garment page opens and, between composition and origin, it asks “Not taking it? Tell us why”.

One tap and five options: **size, fit, price, colour or quality**. No sign-up, no email, nothing to download. We don’t store who they are: only the reason, linked to the garment.

## What changes when you know

One reason is an anecdote. Forty reasons for the same garment are a decision:

- If most say **size**, the problem isn’t the design: review the size curve or the pattern.
- If they say **fit**, the pattern doesn’t work on the body, however good it looks on the hanger.
- If they say **price**, test a change in some stores or on some days and compare with the rest.
- If they say **quality**, it’s a conversation with the supplier, with data in hand.

## Another size without leaving the room

Often the customer doesn’t leave because they dislike the garment, but because they don’t want to get dressed, go out and look for another size. From the same page they can ask for it, and the store gets the alert straight away. It’s a sale that isn’t lost and also a data point: which sizes are requested and which weren’t there.

## No personal data

All of this works without knowing who anyone is. There are no cameras or microphones, and taps on the garment page are never linked to a purchase or a person. To decide what to order next season you don’t need to know who tried on the T-shirt. You need to know why they didn’t take it.
